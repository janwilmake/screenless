the problem

Agents made writing code cheap. They made deciding expensive.

You brought in agents to move faster — not to spend every morning at a screen reviewing code nobody wrote and unblocking the ones that got stuck. But that is the job now, and every part of it waits on a person at a keyboard.

The queue outruns the team

Overnight your agents open more work than anyone reads before standup. It carries to tomorrow, and tomorrow it is bigger — for everyone at once.

The decisions stay stuck in chairs

The calls only a person can make — is this the right scope, does this ship, who picks this up — wait for someone to be at a keyboard, in a thread, in a meeting. So they wait.

A phone needs no screen

A voice line can reach anyone on the team, collect what they decide, and hand it straight to the agents that do the work. That is the one medium in this workflow that isn't a screen.

the line

One number. Your whole team, and every agent behind them.

screenless gives your team a phone line wired to the terminals it runs. It goes both ways: anyone can ring in, and any command can ring out.

Ring in

A teammate calls the line and speaks — a request, a decision, an idea. It is transcribed and routed to whoever's terminal is watching: the caller's own first, any teammate's otherwise, and a queue that holds up to a week when nobody is. Their agent picks it up and acts.

Ring out

screenless call "…" --to alex@team.com, or --all. Dispatch a briefing to one teammate or the whole team, and each conversation lands back in a terminal. It only ever dials verified numbers on your own team.

Watch

Everyone runs screenless watch in a terminal. Calls route to exactly one, so a team drains its queue together — and a teammate's request arrives marked as theirs, weighed before it runs with your access.

the boundary

The voice on the phone cannot touch your code. On purpose.

A hosted service cannot have your team's MCPs, logged-in browsers and Claude subscriptions without asking for credentials nobody should hand over. So it doesn't ask. It takes decisions and gives them back to a terminal that already has the access — never to us.

what the line does
  • Collects what a person says, and only what a person can decide
  • Routes each conversation to one teammate's terminal
  • Marks a teammate's request as theirs, to weigh before it runs
  • Hands the transcript back to a machine on your team
what it will never do
  • Hold a token, a key or a repo credential
  • Merge, close, comment or push from the call itself
  • Keep your source on our servers
  • Reach a number that is not verified on your own team

before you paste that command

The things you were about to ask.

Does my code leave my machine?

No. The reading happens locally, through MCPs you already connected. What reaches us is the short brief the call is placed from and the transcript of what you said back — both deleted within days.

Which coding agents does it work with?

Whatever opens the pull requests — Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, a human on a bad day. The loop reads your repo and your tracker, not your agent. Claude Code is what runs the skills, so you need that.

What if I don't pick up at eight?

Nothing is lost. The undecided items roll into tomorrow's call, and you can ring the line any time to leave a request or your decisions as a voice note — no menu, just a beep, and it lands transcribed in your team's terminal. screenless settings --pause stops the morning call for as long as you want it stopped.

What if there's nothing worth asking about?

Then it doesn't call. A drained queue ends the call early; an empty one never starts it. A phone that rings every morning regardless is a phone you stop answering.

Can I read the install script first?

Please do — it's plain bash, right here, and the CLI it fetches is two files with no dependencies. All of it is on GitHub, MIT.

Will it call my country?

Twenty-eight so far, across Europe, the Americas, Asia-Pacific and South Africa. screenless setup tells you in the first ten seconds if yours isn't one of them, before any card is asked for.

Do I have to talk to it in English?

Ten languages, chosen at setup, and it will follow you if you switch mid-sentence. Voices were picked on measured latency, so it answers at conversation speed rather than waiting a beat.

What does cancelling look like?

There is nothing to cancel — it is pay-as-you-go, not a subscription. Stop calling and you stop spending; leftover credit just sits there, and there is nothing to unsubscribe from.

Can a teammate misuse the line?

A request from a teammate runs on someone else's terminal, so we treat it as untrusted: it arrives marked as theirs, and the agent is told never to read personal data or secrets, never to leave the repo, and never to do anything irreversible without the operator's say-so. The line only ever dials verified numbers on your own team.
