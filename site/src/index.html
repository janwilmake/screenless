how it works

It runs on your machine. That is the whole trick.

03:00

Through the MCPs you already connected — your tracker, your repo, your browser. Nothing new to grant, and your code never leaves.

08:00

08:09

Your agent does the work

The voice has no tools and no credentials. It collects decisions and hangs up; your own loop merges, comments and closes, with the access you already gave it.

the boundary

The voice on the phone cannot touch your code. On purpose.

A hosted service cannot have your MCPs, your logged-in browser and your Claude subscription without asking you for credentials nobody should hand over. So it doesn't ask. It takes decisions and gives them back.

what the call does
  • Reads out what your own agent found overnight
  • Asks only about what it could not settle for you
  • Takes your answer, including "wait, explain that one"
  • Hands the transcript back to your machine
what it will never do
  • Hold a token, a key or a repo credential
  • Merge, close, comment or push
  • Keep your source on our servers
  • Change anything at all while you are on the phone

before you paste that command

The things you were about to ask.

Does my code leave my machine?

No. The reading happens locally, through MCPs you already connected. What reaches us is the short brief the call is placed from and the transcript of what you said back — both deleted within days.

Which coding agents does it work with?

Whatever opens the pull requests — Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, a human on a bad day. The loop reads your repo and your tracker, not your agent. Claude Code is what writes the paper, so you need that.

What if I don't pick up at eight?

Nothing is lost. Decline it and ring the number back whenever suits — same brief, same conversation. screenless settings --pause stops the morning call for as long as you want it stopped.

What if there's nothing worth asking about?

Then it doesn't call. A drained queue ends the call early; an empty one never starts it. A phone that rings every morning regardless is a phone you stop answering.

Can I read the install script first?

Please do — it's plain bash, right here, and the CLI it fetches is two files with no dependencies. All of it is on GitHub, MIT.

Will it call my country?

Twenty-eight so far, across Europe, the Americas, Asia-Pacific and South Africa. screenless setup tells you in the first ten seconds if yours isn't one of them, before any card is asked for.

Do I have to talk to it in English?

Ten languages, chosen at setup, and it will follow you if you switch mid-sentence. Voices were picked on measured latency, so it answers at conversation speed rather than waiting a beat.

What does cancelling look like?

One command into Stripe's own portal. No email, no retention call — which would be a strange thing for us of all products to do.
